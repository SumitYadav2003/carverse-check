WHAT EVERY REPORT CHECKS

Six things worth knowing before you hand over money

Each finding links to the MOT test it came from, so you can see the evidence yourself.

Mileage that went down

A reading lower than the one before can be a typing mistake, a replaced dashboard, or a sign the mileage was wound back.

Warnings that keep coming back

The same advisory at more than one MOT suggests a problem that was never fixed.

Dangerous defects

Anything a tester marked dangerous, so you can ask for proof it was repaired properly.

Long gaps with no MOT

More than about 14 months between tests: the car may have been off the road, or driven without a valid MOT.

How the model compares

How often cars of the same make, model and age failed their MOT, with honest ranges so a small sample can't mislead you.

Mileage against similar cars

Whether this car has been driven much more or much less than others of its model and age.

BE AWARE

What an MOT history can't tell you

An MOT is a safety check on the day of the test. A clean history is a good sign, not a guarantee.

  • Outstanding finance. If money is still owed on the car, it may not be the seller's to sell.
  • Insurance write-offs. A car can be repaired after a write-off and still pass its MOT.
  • Stolen vehicles and plate changes. Check the V5C logbook matches the car and the seller.
  • Service history. Ask for receipts and stamps. MOT tests aren't services.

Common questions

Is it free?

Yes. There's no account, no payment and no sign-up. Type a plate and read the report.

Where does the information come from?

The car's MOT history comes from the DVSA MOT history service. The model comparisons come from DVSA's anonymised data on every MOT test in 2024.

Do you keep the number plates I search?

We don't save them to a database. A plate is kept in memory for up to an hour so repeat visits are quick. Like most websites, the hosting provider's logs record the pages visited, which include the plate, for a limited time. The privacy notice has the details.
